# Journalism

Based on common newsroom standards (verify before publishing, attribute, two independent
sources for anything not confirmed by a primary, separate fact from opinion). A specific
outlet's style guide can be added as its own methodology and takes precedence.

## Settings

- Rumors: leads-only
- Citation style: footnotes
- Translations: offer

## Sources

- In the text: A and B sources; C with attribution in the sentence.
- Forums, imageboards, social media (D): only as quoted statements of an identifiable
  account ("user X wrote on 5ch..."), never as the basis for a fact.
- E: excluded from the text.
- Wikipedia and other wikis: leads only. Find and cite what they cite.

## Confirmation

- Credibility 1 needs a saved primary source, or two independent origins at least one of
  which is rated A or B.
- Anonymous insiders and leaks stay at 3 or lower until a named source or a primary confirms
  them.
- A rumor goes into the text only when the rumor itself is newsworthy: framed as a rumor,
  attributed to its origin, next to the official position (or "did not comment" if that is
  what the sources show).

## In the text

- Attribute every fact ("according to...", "said...") unless a saved primary confirms it.
- Keep fact and analysis visibly apart; your own conclusions are labeled as such.
- Time-sensitive facts carry their date.
- If the people or companies the text is about have not responded to a claim, and the
  sources don't show their response, list it under Gaps in `STATE.md`: the user may need to
  ask for comment before publishing.

## Extra checks

- The headline and the first paragraph rest only on credibility 1-2 claims.
- Shortened quotes keep their meaning. Mark cuts with [...].
